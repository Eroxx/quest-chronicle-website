      Logging a session

      One tap says you played.

      A session is the app's record that you sat down with a game. Log it from the game's page, from a quick search that's one shortcut away, from Siri, or straight from your Home Screen without opening the app at all. Add a line about what happened if you want to. You don't have to.

      The biggest button on every game

      Open a game and Log Session is the first thing your thumb finds. Tap it, add a note if you want one, done.

      Or skip the app entirely: the iPhone widget logs a game in one tap from your Home Screen, and Siri does it from a sentence.

      📱

      From your Home Screen

      The iPhone widget logs a session in one tap without opening the app. Small, medium or large, and you choose which games it shows.

      ⌨️

      From anywhere on the Mac

      ⌘⇧L opens a quick log: type a few letters, pick the game, done. In the library, ⌘L logs the game you're on.

      📝

      A line about it, if you like

      Each session can carry a note about what you beat or where you got stuck, and the Activity tab keeps every one of them in order.

      ➡️

      It moves the game for you

      The first session takes a game out of Backlog and into Questing, because playing it proves you started.

      The lazy option

      Connect Steam or Xbox and stop logging anything.

      It checks every few minutes, and when it sees you've been playing something already in your library it writes the session itself, with how long it lasted. One evening is one session, however many times it looked.

      Steam talks to Valve directly, with your key kept on your device. Xbox goes through xbl.io, an independent service you sign into yourself, because Microsoft offers no other way in. Either way nothing passes through a server of ours, which is also why there's nothing to cancel.

      🎙️

      Or just say so

      "Hey Siri, log Hades in Quest Chronicle." Name the game in the same breath and the app never has to open.

      On iPad

      It runs the Mac's layout, not the phone's.

      Given the width, the iPad doesn't show the phone's app made larger. It shows the desktop one: your filters down the side, the library in the middle, and the game you picked beside it rather than on top of it.

      Attach a keyboard and every shortcut below works there too. Split View and Stage Manager both behave — the library narrows to the single-pane layout and keeps going.

      On the Mac

      Built for a keyboard, not adapted to one.

      A list of several thousand rows you can only drive with a mouse isn't a library, it's an endurance test. Every action has a key, and every key is published in a guide beside the list rather than buried in a menu.

      Yours

      Your library, your iCloud, nobody else's business.

      No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no profile built about you, nothing sold or shared. Your games travel between your devices through your own Apple account and nowhere else.

      The privacy policy says the same thing at length, and it was written by reading every network call in the source rather than from memory.
