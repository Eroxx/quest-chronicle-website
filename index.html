Connect Steam or Xbox and stop logging anything.

Quest Chronicle checks every few minutes, and when it sees you have been playing something in your library it writes the session itself — with how long it lasted. One evening is one session, not six, however many times it looked.

It uses your key, kept on your device. There is no account to make and no server of mine in the middle, which is also why there is nothing to cancel.

🎮

Steam and Xbox

Both take a couple of minutes to set up, and the app walks you through it with the links you need. Everything else works without them.

On iPad, it runs the Mac's layout.

Given the width, the iPad does not show the phone's app made larger. It shows the desktop one: your filters down the side, the library in the middle, and the game you picked beside it rather than on top of it.

Attach a keyboard and every shortcut below works there too. Split View and Stage Manager both behave — the library narrows to the single-pane layout and keeps going.

One tap to log a session.

The moment you want to record playing something is the moment you least want to open an app. The iPhone widget puts your recent games on the home screen — tap one, it's logged, and the widget resets itself. Search from the widget for anything you haven't played lately.

On the Mac it's a keystroke: ⌘L logs the game you have selected, without opening anything. Or ask Siri, and nothing opens at all.

⚡

No app launch

The widget writes the session itself and hands it over next time you open the app. Nothing is lost if you never do.

✏️

Fix it later

Every session can be edited or deleted. A quick log is a starting point, not a commitment.

Five honest answers to "what is this game to me?"

Not a rating. Where the game actually stands with you — and Backlog empties itself, because logging a session on a game proves you started it.

  • Backlog Games you want to play but haven't started yet
  • Questing Games you're actively playing
  • Post-Game Main content is done, but you still have optional goals
  • Trophy Case Games you've completed 100%
  • Abandoned Games you've given up on or decided not to play

Then it tells you what you actually do.

Every figure here is built out of sessions that happened, not out of a rating you gave something once. What you finish and what you abandon. Which consoles and genres really get played, as against the ones you keep buying for. How long it has been since you touched something.

It is the part of a backlog app that usually flatters you. This one doesn't.

Built for a keyboard, not adapted to one.

A list of several thousand rows that can only be driven with a mouse is not a library, it's an endurance test. Every action has a key, and every key is published in a guide that sits beside the list rather than hidden in a menu.
