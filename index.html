      Logging a session

      One tap says you played.

      A session is the app's record that you sat down with a game. Log it from the game's page, from a quick search that's one shortcut away, from Siri, or straight from your Home Screen without opening the app at all. Add a line about what happened if you want to. You don't have to.

      The lazy option

      Connect Steam or Xbox and stop logging anything.

      It checks every few minutes, and when it sees you've been playing something already in your library it writes the session itself, with how long it lasted. One evening is one session, however many times it looked.

      It uses your key, kept on your device. No account to make, no server in the middle — which is also why there's nothing to cancel.

      On iPad

      It runs the Mac's layout, not the phone's.

      Given the width, the iPad doesn't show the phone's app made larger. It shows the desktop one: your filters down the side, the library in the middle, and the game you picked beside it rather than on top of it.

      Attach a keyboard and every shortcut below works there too. Split View and Stage Manager both behave — the library narrows to the single-pane layout and keeps going.

      On the Mac

      Built for a keyboard, not adapted to one.

      A list of several thousand rows you can only drive with a mouse isn't a library, it's an endurance test. Every action has a key, and every key is published in a guide beside the list rather than buried in a menu.
