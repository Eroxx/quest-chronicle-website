knows what you actually played.

Quest Keeper can hold thousands of games and still feel like your library, not a chore. It tracks the details for you — finds your cover art, logs a session in one tap, and never makes you type the same thing twice.

Quest Keeper on macOS

It fills in the covers itself.

A library you cannot recognise at a glance is a list, not a library. Quest Keeper pulls box art automatically — matching on what you typed, not on what you should have typed, so "Froze Horizon 4" still finds Forza. When one source has nothing, it asks another.

🔎

Spelling you don't have to fix

Fuzzy title matching, scored by how much of your search the candidate actually covers — so a near-match wins and a coincidence doesn't.

📚

Import what you already own

Bring in a Steam library or a CSV. Duplicates are caught on the way in, including the same game spelled two different ways.

🖼️

List or covers, one click apart

Names and status when you're organising; a wall of box art when you're deciding what to play. Same games, same filters, different shape.

☁️

The same library everywhere

iCloud sync between Mac and iPhone, continuously and without a Sync button to remember to press.

One tap to log a session.

The moment you want to record playing something is the moment you least want to open an app. The iPhone widget puts your recent games on the home screen — tap one, it's logged, and the widget resets itself. Search from the widget for anything you haven't played lately.

On the Mac it's a keystroke: ⌘L logs the game you have selected, without opening anything.

⚡

No app launch

The widget writes the session itself and hands it over next time you open the app. Nothing is lost if you never do.

✏️

Fix it later

Every session can be edited or deleted. A quick log is a starting point, not a commitment.

Built for a keyboard, not adapted to one.

A list of several thousand rows that can only be driven with a mouse is not a library, it's an endurance test. Every action has a key, and every key is published in a guide that sits beside the list rather than hidden in a menu.

Make it look like yours.

A shelf of themes, each with its own mood rather than its own tint — and every one of them checked for contrast, so nothing goes unreadable to make something else look good. Turn on shuffle and the app picks a new one each launch, light or dark.
